## Why Your Video May Need an MP4 Version

A file extension does not tell the whole story. MP4, MKV, MOV, WebM, AVI, and FLV are containers, while H.264, VP9, HEVC, AAC, Opus, and similar names are codecs inside those containers. If an app rejects your file, the container, the codec, or both may be the reason.

If your WebM plays in Chrome but fails on an iPhone, or your MOV from a phone will not embed cleanly on a website, converting the file to H.264 + AAC MP4 gives you the safest practical playback target. It does not make every old device perfect, but it avoids the most common compatibility trap: a familiar file name with unsupported video or audio inside.

GrepCut is useful when you want a shareable MP4 without sending private footage to a remote converter. You can handle screen recordings, exports from editing apps, camera clips, and downloaded video files directly on your device.

The goal is not to create a magic format. The goal is to give your file the MP4 container and codec combination that browsers and devices are most likely to understand.

## What Changes During MP4 Conversion

When you convert a video to MP4 in GrepCut, the output is not just renamed. Your browser creates a new file using an MP4 container, H.264 video, and AAC audio. That matters because an .mp4 extension alone does not guarantee compatibility.

You may notice that high-resolution files, HEVC clips, or long recordings take longer to process. That is expected because transcoding uses your local CPU rather than a remote server. The tradeoff is privacy: your video does not need to leave your device.

- **Container**: The final file is packaged as MP4, a common delivery container for video.
- **Video**: The video stream is encoded as H.264 (AVC), a widely supported playback codec.
- **Audio**: The audio stream is encoded as AAC for broad device and browser support.
- **Location**: The conversion runs locally in your browser instead of uploading your file to a server.

## Video to MP4 Converter FAQ

### Can you convert WebM, MKV, MOV, AVI, or FLV to MP4?

Yes. You can choose a common video file such as WebM, MKV, MOV, AVI, or FLV and convert it to an MP4 output with H.264 video and AAC audio.

### Will your video file be uploaded?

No. The conversion runs locally in your browser. Your source video is not sent to a server for processing.

### Why does an MP4 sometimes still fail to play?

MP4 is a container, not a single codec. If your MP4 contains a video or audio codec that an app does not support, the file can still fail. GrepCut targets H.264 video and AAC audio because that combination is broadly supported.

### Will converting your video to MP4 be lossless?

No. This is transcoding, not a lossless remux. The output is designed for compatibility with standard H.264 + AAC MP4 playback, so quality can change slightly from the source.

### Why does WebM often need conversion before sharing?

WebM can work well in some browsers and workflows, but it can still cause playback or upload problems in Safari, iOS apps, older devices, and tools that expect MP4. Converting to MP4 can make your file easier to share.

### Can you convert a video to MP4 on a phone?

Yes, as long as your browser and device have enough processing power and memory for the file. Large or high-resolution videos may take longer because your phone does the conversion locally.

### Does the converter remove audio or subtitles?

The MP4 output is focused on standard H.264 video and AAC audio compatibility. Do not rely on it to preserve advanced container features such as multiple audio tracks, subtitle streams, menus, or attachments.
