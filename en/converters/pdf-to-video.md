# Convert PDF to Video Free Online

Convert multi-page PDF documents into a video slideshow. Processes entirely in your browser without uploading to any server.

HTML: https://grepcut.com/en/converters/pdf-to-video

## How to Convert PDF to Video in Your Browser

1. **Select your PDF**: Drop your PDF into the converter area, or choose it with the file picker.
2. **Choose slide timing**: Pick how long each page should stay on screen. You can use slow review presets, standard slideshow pacing, or fast frame-rate playback.
3. **Render pages as frames**: GrepCut renders each PDF page to a canvas locally and turns those rendered pages into a continuous video sequence.
4. **Download your MP4**: Save the finished H.264 MP4 slideshow to your device. Your PDF is not uploaded to a server.

The conversion happens in your browser using PDF.js, canvas rendering, and WebCodecs. That means your document stays on your device while you create a shareable MP4.

## Why Turn Your PDF Pages into an MP4 Slideshow?

A PDF works well when you want someone to read or print a document. It becomes less convenient when you want to show the same pages on a TV, upload them to a video platform, or send a simple preview that plays automatically.

When you convert your PDF to video, every page becomes a frame in an MP4 slideshow. You can turn a slide deck, visual report, portfolio, lesson handout, comic layout, or page-by-page review file into something that plays in a normal video player.

This is especially useful when you already have a finished PDF and do not want to rebuild the file in PowerPoint, Keynote, or a video editor just to make a basic slideshow.

## Choose the Slide Timing That Matches Your PDF

Your video length depends on two things: how many pages your PDF has and how long each page stays visible. A 20-page PDF at **1 FPS** becomes a 20-second MP4. The same file at **1 per minute** becomes a 20-minute video.

For narration, study, or slow review, choose **1 per 5 minutes**, **1 per 2 minutes**, or **1 per minute**. These presets give you time to explain a slide, read a dense page, or pause during a lesson.

For a normal slideshow preview, try **2 per minute**, **4 per minute**, **1 per 10 seconds**, **1 per 5 seconds**, or **1 FPS**. For a fast page scan, flipbook, or reel-style preview, use **8 FPS**, **24 FPS**, **30 FPS**, or **60 FPS**.

Need the reverse workflow? Use the [MP4 to PDF converter](/converters/mp4-to-pdf) to turn video frames into a printable storyboard or flipbook.

## Slow Review vs Fast Playback

| Your goal | Recommended presets | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Narrate a presentation or lesson | 1 per minute to 1 per 5 minutes | Each page stays visible long enough for explanation, reading, or voiceover |
| Preview a deck or document | 2 per minute to 1 FPS | Balanced pacing for meetings, approvals, demos, and quick sharing |
| Scan a long PDF quickly | 8 FPS to 60 FPS | A short MP4 that moves through pages rapidly |
| Upload to a phone or video platform | 1 FPS to 30 FPS | A standard MP4 file with H.264 video for broad playback |

Total video length equals your page count multiplied by the time each page stays on screen.

## What Happens to Your File During Conversion?

Your PDF is read locally by your browser. GrepCut renders each page as an image-like canvas frame, then encodes those frames into an MP4 video.

The output is a silent H.264 MP4 because a PDF contains pages, not audio. If you need music, narration, trimming, or more advanced editing, create the slideshow first and then edit the exported video in GrepCut.

Very large PDFs or very long slow-paced videos can take more time because your own device is doing the rendering and encoding work. There is no server-side upload limit, but your browser, memory, and CPU still matter.

## PDF to Video Converter FAQ

### Can you convert a PDF presentation to video?

Yes. Drop your PDF presentation into GrepCut, choose a slide timing preset, and export it as an MP4 slideshow. Each PDF page becomes one part of the video.

### Will your PDF be uploaded?

No. GrepCut processes your PDF in your browser, so your file stays on your device during conversion. This is useful when your PDF contains private, internal, or client material.

### How long will your PDF video be?

Video length depends on your page count and timing preset. With **1 FPS**, a 12-page PDF becomes a 12-second video. With **1 per minute**, the same PDF becomes a 12-minute video.

### Can you make each PDF page stay on screen for 5 seconds?

Yes. Choose the **1 per 5 seconds** preset. You can also use slower presets such as **1 per minute**, or faster presets such as **24 FPS**, depending on how quickly you want the pages to advance.

### What frame-rate presets can you choose?

You can choose from **1 per 5 minutes**, **1 per 2 minutes**, **1 per minute**, **2 per minute**, **4 per minute**, **1 per 10 seconds**, **1 per 5 seconds**, **1 FPS**, **8 FPS**, **24 FPS**, **30 FPS**, and **60 FPS**.

### Does the exported MP4 include sound?

No. The PDF to video converter creates a silent MP4 slideshow because a normal PDF only contains pages. You can add music or narration later in the GrepCut editor.

### Is there a page limit for your PDF?

There is no server upload limit because the conversion runs locally. In practice, very large PDFs depend on your device performance, available memory, browser support, and the timing preset you choose.

### Why use MP4 instead of keeping the file as a PDF?

Use PDF when you want reading, printing, or annotation. Use MP4 when you want automatic playback, a shareable slideshow, a video preview, or a file that can be posted to video platforms.
