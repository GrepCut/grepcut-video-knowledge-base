## WebM vs. MP4: The Useful Difference

| Aspect | WebM | MP4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is best at | Efficient web playback, browser-based experiences, and workflows where you control the player. | Sharing, editing, uploading, and playback on the widest range of everyday devices. |
| Common video codecs | VP8, VP9, and AV1, modern codecs built for efficient delivery. | Most often H.264/AVC; newer MP4 workflows may also use HEVC/H.265 or AV1. |
| Common audio codecs | Opus and Vorbis, both popular in web media. | AAC is the familiar default for consumer video. |
| Browser playback | Strong in modern browsers, though compatibility can vary by browser, device, and codec. | H.264/AAC MP4 is broadly supported across desktop and mobile browsers. |
| Apps and devices | Can be hit-or-miss in stock players, older Apple software, some TVs, and enterprise tools. | Usually plays or imports without extra software on phones, computers, TVs, consoles, and editors. |
| Choose it when | You are publishing for the web or need features such as transparent WebM overlays. | You need the least surprising file for another person, platform, or device. |

Think of WebM as a capable native of the web, and MP4 as the passport your video needs when it is headed out into the world.

## A Few Format Facts Worth Knowing

WebM arrived in 2010 as an open, web-focused alternative in a video world dominated by H.264. Its efficient VP8, VP9, and AV1 codecs helped make high-quality browser video more practical.

MP4 is not a codec; it is a container, the file wrapper that holds video, audio, subtitles, and metadata. Its strength is familiarity: decades of devices and applications know what to do with it.

Many large services can accept several formats, but still recommend H.264 video with AAC audio in an MP4 container. It is the lowest-friction starting point for their processing systems and for the people who will watch the result.

If your source WebM uses transparency, keep the original. Standard H.264 MP4 is designed for opaque video, so an MP4 share copy gains compatibility but does not preserve an alpha channel.

## Common questions

### Will converting WebM to MP4 reduce quality?

A WebM-to-MP4 conversion re-encodes the video, so it is technically lossy. With sensible settings and the same resolution and frame rate, the difference is typically hard to notice in screen recordings, webcam clips, and social video.

### Is my video uploaded anywhere?

No. GrepCut processes the conversion in your browser with WebCodecs and WebAssembly. Your file stays on your device rather than being sent to a third-party conversion server.

### Why does my WebM not play on an iPhone or in my editor?

Support for WebM and its common VP9/Opus codecs varies between apps and devices. H.264/AAC MP4 has much broader support, so converting often resolves import failures, black video, or playback problems.

### Will I keep transparency when converting?

Usually not. WebM can carry transparency, but standard H.264 MP4 workflows do not. Keep the original WebM for overlays and export an MP4 copy when broad playback matters more than alpha transparency.

### Which browsers work best?

Recent Chromium-based browsers such as Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, and Arc generally offer the smoothest experience. Firefox and current Safari may also work, though performance and codec support can differ by version and device.

### Why not upload the WebM directly to social media?

Some platforms accept WebM, but many still prefer or recommend H.264/AAC MP4. Converting first gives you a known, widely compatible file and more control before the platform applies its own compression.
