## How to Convert MP4 to MOV in Your Browser

1. **Drop in your MP4**: Drag your MP4 file into GrepCut, or choose it with the file picker. The video stays on your device from the start.
2. **Let GrepCut inspect the streams**: GrepCut checks the video and audio inside the MP4. If the file already uses H.264 video with AAC audio, it can usually be remuxed into a MOV wrapper without re-encoding.
3. **Save your MOV**: Download the finished QuickTime MOV for iMovie, Final Cut, QuickTime Player, or another Apple-focused workflow.

A compatible MP4 can become a MOV quickly because only the container changes. If your source uses a stream that is not safe for MOV, GrepCut converts the main playable track to H.264/AAC locally instead.

## Why Your MP4 May Only Need a MOV Wrapper

MP4 and MOV are close relatives. MP4 is based on the ISO Base Media File Format family, which grew out of Apple's QuickTime format, so a simple MP4 to MOV conversion is often a container job rather than a visual rebuild.

That distinction matters for your quality. When your MP4 already contains H.264 video and AAC audio, GrepCut can copy those streams into a MOV file. Your frames and audio samples are preserved instead of being compressed again.

This is why changing the file extension is not the same as converting the file. A real remux writes a valid MOV structure around the media streams so Apple apps can read the file as a QuickTime movie.

Use this when your video already looks right, but the app you need prefers `.mov` over `.mp4`.

## When GrepCut Converts Instead of Remuxing

If your MP4 contains a codec that does not belong in a safe MOV export, GrepCut does not pretend the file is compatible. It transcodes the main playable track to H.264/AAC so your output has a better chance of opening in Apple tools.

This fallback can take longer because your browser has to decode and encode the media. It may also change the compressed video slightly, as any real transcode can. The benefit is that you get a usable MOV instead of a failed conversion.

Your browser and device still matter. Large videos need enough memory, storage, and processing power, especially when transcoding is required.

## MP4 vs MOV for Your Apple Workflow

| Aspect | MP4 | MOV |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Best fit | Web sharing, broad device support, uploads to platforms | QuickTime, iMovie, Final Cut, and Apple-first editing workflows |
| Typical compatible streams | H.264 or HEVC video with AAC audio | H.264, HEVC, ProRes, or other QuickTime-friendly streams depending on the app |
| Fast conversion case | Your source already has H.264/AAC inside the MP4 | The same streams can be copied into a MOV wrapper |
| When quality can change | Only if the media is re-encoded | Only if GrepCut must transcode instead of remuxing |

Choose MOV when your next step is an Apple app. Keep MP4 when your next step is broad sharing on the web.

## MP4 to MOV Converter FAQ

### Can you convert MP4 to MOV without losing quality?

Yes, when your MP4 already contains compatible H.264 video and AAC audio. GrepCut remuxes those streams into a MOV wrapper, so the video frames and audio samples are copied instead of re-encoded. If the file needs transcoding, quality can change slightly because a new H.264/AAC version is created.

### Can you just rename an MP4 file to MOV?

Renaming only changes the filename. It does not rewrite the container, fix metadata, or change the codec inside your file. GrepCut creates an actual MOV output by remuxing compatible streams or transcoding when needed.

### Will your MP4 work in QuickTime Player after conversion?

A QuickTime-safe H.264/AAC MOV is more likely to open in Apple playback tools than a random MP4 with unsupported streams. No converter can guarantee every receiving app will accept every file, but GrepCut avoids the common mistake of copying unsafe codecs into a MOV wrapper.

### Does MOV make your file better for iMovie or Final Cut?

MOV can be a better fit when your next step is an Apple editing workflow. For simple compatibility, a remuxed or H.264/AAC MOV may be enough. For heavy professional editing, you may still need a different intermediate format outside this converter's scope.

### Why did GrepCut transcode your MP4 instead of remuxing it?

GrepCut transcodes when the streams inside your MP4 are not safe to copy into MOV, or when your browser cannot decode them reliably. That slower path gives you a playable H.264/AAC MOV instead of a broken file.

### Is your private video uploaded anywhere?

No. The converter runs in your browser, so your selected file stays on your device. That makes it suitable for personal clips, client previews, screen recordings, and other videos you do not want to send to a server.

### Is there a file size limit?

There is no server upload limit because there is no upload. Your practical limit is your own browser, memory, storage, and battery. A large remux can be fast, while a large transcode can take noticeably longer.
