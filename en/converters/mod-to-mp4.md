## Why Your MOD File May Not Play Today

A MOD file usually comes from an older JVC, Panasonic, or Canon tapeless camcorder. It is not a modern web video format. It is commonly a standard-definition MPEG Program Stream saved with a .mod extension, which is why a clip that worked with the original camcorder software may fail on your phone, tablet, or browser today.

Converting MOD to MP4 gives your footage a format that modern devices understand more easily. GrepCut transcodes the MPEG-2 video to H.264 and the audio to AAC, then wraps both streams in an MP4 container that is far more practical for sharing, previewing, uploading, and editing.

This is especially useful when you are rescuing family tapes, vacation footage, school recordings, or old SD card clips. You can turn the file into a playable MP4 without sending personal video to a third-party upload converter.

## What Happens During MOD to MP4 Conversion

MOD is a container problem and a codec problem at the same time. The old file may contain MPEG-2 video with AC-3 or MPEG audio, while your target MP4 is designed for broad modern playback with H.264 video and AAC audio.

GrepCut does not only rename the extension. It uses FFmpeg.wasm to read the MOD input, decode the camcorder streams, encode a fresh H.264/AAC output, and package the result as MP4.

Because this is transcoding, it is not lossless. Keep your original .mod file if it is your archive copy, and use the MP4 as the convenient playback and sharing version.

If you only rename .mod to .mpg, some desktop players may open it, but that does not give you the same phone-friendly MP4 output.

## Why Old MOD Clips Can Look Stretched

Some MOD camcorders recorded 4:3 and 16:9 clips using standard-definition frame sizes such as 720×480 or 720×576. The intended display shape can depend on aspect ratio flags and, in some camera workflows, nearby .moi metadata files.

That is why a clip can look fine in one player but squeezed or stretched in another. If your source archive includes matching .moi files, keep them with the originals even if you only upload the .mod file to the converter.

The MP4 you download is meant for easier modern playback, but your original files remain important if you ever need to revisit dates, camera metadata, or aspect ratio troubleshooting.

## MOD to MP4 Converter FAQ

### Can you convert MOD to MP4 without uploading your video?

Yes. GrepCut runs the conversion in your browser with FFmpeg.wasm, so your MOD file stays on your device instead of being uploaded to a server.

### Will your MOD file lose quality during conversion?

The MP4 is transcoded, not losslessly remuxed. GrepCut aims to preserve visible quality while making a practical H.264/AAC MP4, but you should keep your original .mod file as the archive.

### Can you just rename MOD to MPG instead?

Sometimes renaming .mod to .mpg helps a desktop player recognize the MPEG Program Stream. It does not create a modern H.264/AAC MP4, so it may still be awkward on phones, browsers, and sharing platforms.

### Why does your MOD video look stretched after conversion?

Old MOD camcorders sometimes relied on aspect ratio flags or .moi metadata for 4:3 and 16:9 display. If your clip looks squeezed or stretched, compare it with the original camera playback and keep the matching .moi files with your archive.

### What is a MOI file next to your MOD file?

A .moi file is a small metadata file used by some JVC and Canon camcorders. It can store information such as date, duration, audio details, and aspect ratio data. You usually convert the .mod video file, but you should keep the .moi file for your archive.

### Why is browser MOD conversion slower than a desktop app?

FFmpeg.wasm runs through WebAssembly in your browser and uses your device CPU and memory. That keeps your video private and installation-free, but long MPEG-2 camcorder recordings can take noticeably longer.

### Can you convert damaged or very large MOD files?

You can try, but browser memory is the limit. If your file is damaged, extremely large, or from an unusual camera workflow, conversion may fail before GrepCut can produce a complete MP4.
