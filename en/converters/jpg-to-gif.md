## How to Convert JPG Images to GIF in Your Browser

1. **Add your JPG files**: Drop one or more .jpg or .jpeg files into the converter, or choose them with the file picker. A single image becomes a one-frame GIF. Multiple images become an animation.
2. **Check the frame order**: Review the Frame order list before you convert. New files are sorted alphabetically by filename, so numbered names such as 001-frame.jpg and 002-frame.jpg make the default order predictable.
3. **Reorder or remove frames**: Drag frames into the playback order you want, use the up and down buttons, or remove any frame that should not appear in the final loop.
4. **Choose the frame rate**: Pick 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, or 24 FPS. Ten FPS is the default balance for short loops. Lower FPS works well for slideshows. Higher FPS makes quick motion smoother and usually increases file size.
5. **Convert and download**: Click Convert to GIF, then download the finished animation. For large outputs, enable Save directly to disk in Chrome or Edge so the GIF is written to your drive instead of staying in browser memory.

GrepCut loads your JPG frames on canvas, reduces each frame to a 256-color GIF palette, and encodes the animation locally in your browser. Your images are not uploaded to GrepCut servers.

## When JPG to GIF Is the Right Workflow

Use JPG to GIF when you already have still frames on disk and want one looping file. Your source could be a stop-motion test, a product rotation, storyboard panels, a UI walkthrough, exported timelapse frames, or a few before-and-after photos.

This saves you from rebuilding a simple frame sequence in video software. You can add the images, put them in the correct order, set how fast they play, and download a GIF that autoplays in places where a still JPEG would not show motion.

You can also convert one JPG into a still GIF when a form, legacy tool, email template, or older workflow expects .gif instead of .jpg. It will not add motion, but it gives you the required container.

## Why Frame Rate Changes the Feel and File Size

Your FPS choice controls how many frames are shown each second. If you have 70 images and choose 10 FPS, the loop plays for about seven seconds. If you choose 5 FPS, the same sequence lasts about fourteen seconds.

For a slow photo sequence, 2-6 FPS often feels clearer because each frame stays visible. For quick UI motion or a product spin, 12-15 FPS can look smoother. Use 20-24 FPS only when the motion needs it and you can accept a larger file.

GIF stores timing as frame delays rather than a video-style frame rate, so some apps and browsers may round very small delays. After you download the GIF, test it in the place where you plan to share it.

## What to Expect When JPEG Photos Become GIF Frames

JPEG is designed for full-color still photos, while GIF is palette-based. When your JPG photos become GIF frames, smooth gradients, shadows, skies, and airbrushed artwork may show banding or speckled dithering because each frame is limited to 256 colors.

Flat graphics, icons, UI captures, product shots on clean backgrounds, and high-contrast sequences usually survive the conversion better than soft photographic gradients. If you need rich color or long smooth motion, a video format may be a better final format than GIF.

Large dimensions, many frames, and high FPS all push the output size up. If your GIF is too large, remove duplicate frames, lower the FPS, reduce the image size before converting, or use Save directly to disk for memory-heavy exports.

## JPG to GIF Converter FAQ

### Can you turn multiple JPG images into one animated GIF?

Yes. Add multiple .jpg or .jpeg files, check their order, choose an FPS preset, and convert. GrepCut turns the ordered frames into one looping GIF in your browser.

### Can you convert a single JPG to GIF?

Yes. A single JPG becomes a one-frame GIF. Use this when your destination specifically asks for a GIF file, not when you need animation.

### How do you keep your frames in the right order?

Name your files with leading numbers, such as 001.jpg, 002.jpg, and 003.jpg. GrepCut sorts new files alphabetically by default, and you can still drag frames into a custom order before converting.

### What FPS should you choose for a JPG sequence?

**10 FPS** is the default and works for many short loops. Use **2-6 FPS** when you want a slideshow feel, **12-15 FPS** for smoother motion, and **20-24 FPS** only when speed matters more than output size.

### Will your photo quality drop when you convert JPG to GIF?

Usually, yes. GIF supports a maximum of **256 colors per frame**, so full-color photos can show banding, dithering, or flatter gradients. Simple graphics and high-contrast frames usually look cleaner.

### Why is your GIF file so large?

GIF size grows with frame count, dimensions, and FPS. Remove frames you do not need, lower the FPS, resize your JPGs before converting, or enable **Save directly to disk** when the browser warns about memory.

### What happens if your JPG frames have different sizes?

The GIF uses the width and height of the **first frame** in the ordered list. Later frames are scaled with a cover-style crop to fill that same canvas without distortion.

### Are your JPG images uploaded during conversion?

No. GrepCut converts your images locally in your browser tab. Your frames are not uploaded to GrepCut servers, which is useful for private photos, client drafts, and internal work.
