## Why Your iPhone Video May Not Play on Windows or in an Editor

Your iPhone can record very efficient video: HEVC / H.265 inside a MOV or MP4 container. That keeps your camera roll smaller, but the same file can fail when you move it to a Windows PC, send it to an Android device, or import it into an editor that expects a different codec path.

If your file opens as audio only, shows a blank preview, asks for an HEVC extension, or imports as offline media, the problem is usually not the .mov name by itself. The real issue is the codec inside the container. Converting to **MP4 with H.264 video and AAC audio** gives you the most predictable playback target.

GrepCut does a full transcode for this converter. It does not pretend that an iPhone HEVC file can always be fixed by renaming or remuxing, because your target device still has to decode the video stream.

HEVC is efficient, but H.264 is still the safer compatibility choice when your next app cannot read the original file. GrepCut decodes the iPhone video frames, re-encodes them, and writes a new container with new audio and video streams.

That conversion is not lossless. Your converted file is a practical sharing copy, not a replacement for the original recording. Keep the iPhone MOV or MP4 as your master if the footage matters.

WebM and MKV targets are useful when you control the next playback environment, but they are not as universal as MP4 on phones, TVs, and upload forms. Use them when your web player, archive tool, or downstream pipeline specifically benefits from VP9 + Opus.

## iPhone Video Converter FAQ

### Why will your iPhone MOV not play on Windows?

Your MOV may contain HEVC / H.265 video. Some Windows setups can open the container but cannot decode the video stream, so you may see a blank preview, hear only audio, or get a codec prompt. Converting to **MP4 with H.264 + AAC** is usually the safest fix.

### Can you convert iPhone HEVC to MP4 without uploading it?

Yes. GrepCut runs the conversion locally in your browser with WebCodecs and Mediabunny. Your file is processed on your device rather than uploaded to a cloud converter.

### Should you choose MP4 or MOV for iPhone footage?

Choose **MP4** when you need broad playback on Windows, Android, TVs, browsers, and upload sites. Choose **MOV** when your next step is a Mac or a QuickTime-first editor that expects a .mov container.

### Will converting your iPhone video reduce quality?

Yes. This converter performs a full transcode from HEVC to another codec, so it is not a lossless copy. Use the converted file for playback, sharing, or editing compatibility, and keep the original iPhone file if you need a master archive.

### Why not just rename MOV to MP4?

Renaming only changes the filename. It does not change the codec inside the file. If your playback problem comes from HEVC / H.265 support, a real transcode to H.264 or VP9 is the fix.

### Can you use this for iPhone videos that import as audio only?

Yes, if the audio-only import is caused by your editor failing to decode HEVC video. Convert the clip to MP4 with H.264 + AAC first, then import the new file into the editor.

### Is WebM better than MP4 for your iPhone video?

Not for general sharing. WebM is useful for web delivery and Chromium-friendly playback, but MP4 with H.264 + AAC is still the safer choice when you do not control the viewer's device or app.

### Can you convert 4K iPhone HEVC video in the browser?

You can, but 4K HEVC takes more CPU, memory, and time than a short 1080p clip. Keep the tab open during conversion and expect performance to depend on your device.
